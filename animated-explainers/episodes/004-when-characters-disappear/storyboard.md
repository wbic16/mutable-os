# When characters disappear

*Annotation boundary management during deletion*

Concepts · Volume I — The Nature of Text · Episode 004 · 56 seconds

> It was a bright and beautiful morning.

Annotation A is a typographic range on **bright and beautiful**. Its endpoints are inclusive. Ask a viewer to follow the start pin from the first **b** to the second **b**. At the end of the slide the index reads 9 again, but a different character Cell now occupies that position. The annotation still has identity A.

## How to read the drawing

The upper sentence preserves the reading context. The detail strip enlarges the relevant Cells. A centred dot makes a space visible there. The numbers give current zero-based positions. Coral identifies the deletion. A teal pin follows a surviving Cell. An amber travelling pin shows a reference changing from one object to another.

The three independent trials restore the original example through a brief fade marked “Original sentence”. The last three edits continue from “bright”. They leave **g**, then remove it. Two spaces remain on either side of the gap because they lay outside the original typographic range.

The sequence illustrates an ordinary contiguous range whose definition requires at least one character. Its maintenance rule says where the typographic mark applies. Semantic judgements about the resulting sentence and policies for point annotations are outside this example.
